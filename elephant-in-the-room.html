Taxpayer accountability

The Elephant in the Room.

Strathroy-Caradoc faces connected financial, infrastructure, growth, and accountability pressures. Residents deserve clear facts, transparent financing, current plans, and Council decisions grounded in evidence.

These issues affect property taxes, utility rates, debt, service reliability, growth decisions, and the long-term condition of the infrastructure residents rely on. The figures below show the scale and type of financial pressure—not one combined bill to taxpayers.

What is at stake?

Strathroy-Caradoc’s financial pressures are not all the same. Some affect property taxes, some are paid through utility rates, and others involve debt, grants, development charges, reserves, or unresolved financial information.

Issues taxpayers deserve clear answers on

01

A current infrastructure plan is overdue

The Municipality needs a current Asset Management Plan showing asset condition, service levels, lifecycle costs, risks, and long-term funding needs for roads, bridges, drainage, buildings, fleet, water, wastewater, and stormwater.

My priority: Infrastructure and municipal capacity and Financial responsibility and taxpayer protection.

02

Major projects need complete financing disclosure

Before taxpayers are committed to major capital projects, Council should publish total cost, scope, debt, reserves, grants, operating costs, debt-service impact, and contingency.

My priority: Transparent and accountable government and Financial responsibility and taxpayer protection.

03

Legal and contingent financial risks need responsible reporting

Litigation, planning appeals, insurance deductibles, and other contingent liabilities can create sudden financial pressure. Council should report aggregate legal-risk information as openly as the law permits.

My priority: Transparent and accountable government and Financial responsibility and taxpayer protection.

04

Tax increases need a clear long-term explanation

Residents deserve to know not only what a tax increase funds today, but what it means for future reserves, debt, maintenance obligations, service levels, and affordability.

My priority: Financial responsibility and taxpayer protection.

05

Roads, bridges, drainage, and stormwater need sustained renewal

Safe roads, bridges, culverts, sidewalks, and drainage systems require a clear long-term renewal program. Deferring maintenance can turn manageable work into expensive reconstruction.

My priority: Infrastructure and municipal capacity.

06

Water and wastewater compliance must be costed openly

Residents need clear reporting on water and wastewater projects, financing, construction schedules, utility-rate impacts, development-charge recovery, and the risks of delayed work.

My priority: Infrastructure and municipal capacity and Financial responsibility and taxpayer protection.

07

Service changes and user fees require value-for-money scrutiny

Changes to waste collection, green-bin services, user fees, or contracted services should be explained in plain language, including cost, service standard, alternatives, and benefits.

My priority: Financial responsibility and taxpayer protection and Transparent and accountable government.

08

Community-facility investments need a lifecycle plan

Arenas, parks, fields, and municipal facilities should show their full lifecycle cost, operating impact, funding source, condition need, and priority relative to core services.

My priority: Financial responsibility and taxpayer protection and Infrastructure and municipal capacity.

09

Intermunicipal servicing needs a fair and durable agreement

Shared water, wastewater, stormwater, and growth-related servicing arrangements must be transparent, lawful, financially fair, and durable.

My priority: Transparent government, Taxpayer protection, and Infrastructure capacity.

10

Housing funding and growth commitments must be achievable

External housing funding can benefit the Municipality, but commitments must be matched by realistic planning, servicing, infrastructure, staffing, and financial capacity.

My priority: Responsible planning and land-use decisions, Taxpayer protection, and Infrastructure capacity.

11

Municipal capacity and project management matter

Strong Council decisions require reliable staff capacity, clear communication, disciplined project management, and timely public reporting.

My priority: Transparent and accountable government and Infrastructure and municipal capacity.

12

Employment-land growth must protect taxpayers, farms, and rural communities

Commercial and industrial development can strengthen the tax base, but only when infrastructure costs are recovered fairly and decisions respect agricultural land, rural roads, drainage, and servicing capacity.

My priority: Responsible planning, Protecting agriculture and rural interests, and Infrastructure capacity.

Questions Council should answer publicly

  • When will Council receive and approve a current Asset Management Plan?
  • What is the full financing, debt-service, reserve, and operating-cost impact of each major capital project?
  • What are the current reserve balances, which reserves are in deficit, and what is the repayment plan?
  • How will wastewater compliance, expansion, and debt affect future utility rates?
  • How will Council ensure new growth pays a fair share of the infrastructure it requires?
  • What public reporting will prevent project cost escalation and surprise commitments?

This page summarizes policy and accountability questions drawn from publicly available municipal budgets, plans, reports, meeting materials, and financial disclosures. It is not a finding of legal liability or an allegation of wrongdoing by any individual. Financial figures, project costs, reserve balances, and legal matters should be read with their source dates, scope, and stated limitations in mind.

Clear facts. Responsible decisions. Long-term value.

Strathroy-Caradoc needs Council leadership that reads the material, asks necessary questions, protects taxpayers, and plans for the full cost of today’s decisions.
