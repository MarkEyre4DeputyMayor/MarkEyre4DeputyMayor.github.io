Taxpayer accountability

The Elephant in the Room.

Strathroy-Caradoc faces connected financial, infrastructure, growth, and accountability pressures. Residents deserve clear facts, transparent financing, current plans, and Council decisions grounded in evidence.

These issues affect property taxes, utility rates, debt, service reliability, growth decisions, and the long-term condition of the infrastructure residents rely on. The figures below show the scale and type of financial pressure—not one combined bill to taxpayers.

What is at Stake?

Strathroy-Caradoc’s financial pressures are not all the same. Some affect property taxes, some are paid through utility rates, and others involve debt, grants, development charges, reserves, or information that has not been publicly reconciled.

What These Figures Mean

Infrastructure funding

Council needs a realistic and current plan to renew core assets before deferred work becomes expensive emergency work.

Asset Management Plan

The Municipality needs an updated AMP that clearly shows asset condition, service levels, lifecycle costs, risks, and a long-term funding strategy.

Police Annex financing

The announced project amount and documented debt figure do not fully reconcile. One complete project budget should show total cost, debt, reserves, grants, taxation, and annual debt service.

Roads and reserves

Residents need to know what is funded by grants, reserves, debt, and taxation—and how negative or depleted reserves will be rebuilt.

Wastewater costs

Residents need clear forecasts of rate increases, project costs, debt, compliance deadlines, and growth-related cost recovery.

Legal risk disclosure

Residents do not need confidential legal strategy, but they deserve basic aggregate disclosure of active matters, insurance protection where possible, deductibles, and annual external legal spending.

Issues Taxpayers Deserve Clear Answers On

01

A Current Infrastructure Plan is Overdue

The Municipality needs a current Asset Management Plan showing condition, service levels, lifecycle costs, risks, and long-term funding needs.

My priority: Infrastructure and municipal capacity and Financial responsibility and taxpayer protection.

02

Major Projects Need Complete Financing Disclosure

Before taxpayers are committed, Council should publish total cost, scope, debt, reserves, grants, operating cost, annual debt service, and contingency.

My priority: Transparent and accountable government and Financial responsibility and taxpayer protection.

03

Legal and Contingent Financial Risks Need Responsible Reporting

Public financial statements disclose legal actions but do not provide a clear aggregate public risk profile. Council should report what it can without compromising legal privilege.

My priority: Transparent and accountable government and Financial responsibility and taxpayer protection.

04

Tax Increases Need a Clear Long-Term Explanation

Residents deserve to know what current tax decisions mean for future reserves, debt, maintenance obligations, service levels, and affordability.

My priority: Financial responsibility and taxpayer protection.

05

Water and Wastewater Compliance Must Be Costed Openly

Residents need clear project, financing, construction, rate-impact, debt, and development-charge reporting for essential water and wastewater work.

My priority: Infrastructure and municipal capacity.

06

Growth Must Pay Its Fair Share

Housing and employment growth should be matched by planning, servicing, infrastructure, staffing, and a fair financing plan—not create an unfunded burden for existing residents.

My priority: Responsible planning and land-use decisions, Protecting agriculture and rural interests, and Infrastructure and municipal capacity.

Questions Council Should Answer Publicly

  • When will Council receive and approve a current Asset Management Plan?
  • What is the complete financing, debt-service, reserve, and operating-cost impact of each major project?
  • What are the current reserve balances, which reserves are in deficit, and what is the repayment plan?
  • How will wastewater compliance, expansion, and debt affect future utility rates?
  • How will Council ensure new growth pays a fair share of the infrastructure it requires?
  • What public reporting will prevent project cost escalation and surprise commitments?
