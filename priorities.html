Mark Eyre's Priorities

Practical leadership for Strathroy-Caradoc.

Mark’s priorities are grounded in responsible local government, fair process, long-term thinking, and respect for the people who call Strathroy-Caradoc home.

The Deputy Mayor must be prepared to study the material, listen to residents, understand the municipality’s responsibilities, and make decisions based on evidence and the public interest.

01

Transparent and accountable government

  • Council decisions should be open, well explained, and based on the public record.
  • Residents deserve meaningful opportunities to be heard, without unnecessary procedural barriers.
  • Council members should come to meetings prepared, listen to evidence, ask necessary questions, and explain their decisions clearly.
  • Where competing considerations arise, Council should show how it weighed the relevant evidence, public input, municipal responsibilities, and long-term consequences.
02

Responsible planning and land-use decisions

  • Major land-use decisions must be grounded in planning rules, Official Plan policy, evidence, servicing capacity, and the long-term public interest—not predetermined outcomes or political pressure.
  • Council should consider final staff reports, public input, applicable legislation and planning policy, servicing information, and the full record before making a decision.
  • Where a decision involves competing considerations or a proposal that may depart from established policy, Council must carefully consider the legal framework, evidence, impacts, alternatives, and long-term consequences, and clearly explain its rationale.
  • Official Plan and zoning amendments, especially those involving agricultural or greenfield land, require careful scrutiny and fair process.
  • Residents affected by planning proposals deserve clear information, meaningful opportunity to participate, and respectful consideration.
03

Protecting agriculture and rural interests

  • Agricultural land and rural communities are essential to Strathroy-Caradoc’s identity, economy, and future.
  • Council must understand the real-world impacts that development, traffic, servicing, drainage, and land-use changes can have on farms and rural residents.
  • Decisions affecting agricultural and rural areas should apply sound rural-planning principles, including Minimum Distance Separation requirements where applicable.
  • Where there are competing land-use, infrastructure, environmental, or growth considerations, Council must carefully assess the evidence and impacts on agricultural operations, rural residents, and the municipality’s long-term interests.
  • Rural voices should carry equal weight in municipal decision-making.
04

Financial responsibility and taxpayer protection

  • Every municipal dollar should be spent carefully, transparently, and with clear public value.
  • Procurement, tendering, and purchasing practices must be strong enough to protect taxpayers and prevent avoidable costs.
  • Any exception to normal tendering or purchasing practice should receive careful scrutiny, a clear public explanation, and appropriate documentation.
  • Council must consider immediate costs as well as long-term operating, maintenance, debt, infrastructure, and legal obligations.
  • Decisions should seek to reduce avoidable legal costs and unnecessary taxpayer exposure to financial risk.
  • Council should work with other governments, residents, applicants, and affected parties fairly and openly, seeking practical, lawful, and proportionate resolutions that reduce conflict, financial harm, and mounting legal expenses for taxpayers.
05

Infrastructure and municipal capacity

  • Reliable wastewater, water, roads, drainage, and other core services must be properly planned before growth outpaces municipal capacity.
  • Council must identify capacity constraints early, assess practical alternatives, and understand the lifecycle costs and risks of proposed solutions.
  • Where there are competing demands or limited resources, Council should carefully weigh service needs, affordability, timing, long-term resilience, and impacts on existing residents.
  • Mark will bring an engineer’s discipline to reviewing infrastructure needs, risks, costs, and options.
  • The municipality should choose durable, evidence-based investments rather than costly short-term fixes.

Leadership that is prepared, independent, and focused on the long term.

Mark Eyre is running for Deputy Mayor because Strathroy-Caradoc needs leadership that reads the material, respects the rules, asks difficult questions, and puts sound long-term decisions ahead of political convenience.
