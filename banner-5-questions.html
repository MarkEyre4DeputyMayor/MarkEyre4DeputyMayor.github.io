The Middlesex Banner candidate questionnaire

My Full Answers to the Middlesex Banner

The Middlesex Banner asked candidates five questions about their background, reasons for running, Council’s work, municipal priorities and fair representation.

Below are my complete responses as submitted. I’m sharing them here so residents can read the detail behind my priorities and the approach I would bring to the role of Deputy Mayor.

Mark Eyre — Candidate for Deputy Mayor, Strathroy-Caradoc

Question 1 of 5

Could you briefly introduce yourself, your background, and your connection to Strathroy-Caradoc?

I am a retired Professional Engineer, currently non-practising, with an Executive MBA from the University of Bath in England and experience in business, engineering, water and wastewater projects, infrastructure optimization, regulatory planning, leadership, consulting and financial services.

I moved to Strathroy in 2009 after returning to Canada with my family. I own a hobby farm on Walkers Drive providing a direct connection to rural, agricultural and land-use issues. I coached youth hockey locally for a season.

In the past year I have spent considerable time reviewing municipal planning, governance and public-participation matters. Through that work I have learned how decisions made at Council affect residents, taxpayers and the future of our communities.

I am running to bring practical experience, independent judgment, careful preparation and the time needed to serve Strathroy-Caradoc effectively.

Question 2 of 5

Why are you running for Deputy Mayor, and what would you bring to the role?

I am running because I believe I can be a better and more effective Deputy Mayor. I would not ask residents for their support if I did not believe that.

My experience with a local planning application showed me how important careful, independent review has become. Provincial changes now prevent most ordinary residents and neighbours from appealing approved Official Plan and zoning amendments, even when they participated in the process. For many people, Council is effectively their last meaningful decision point.

As Deputy Mayor, I would have a vote both locally and at Middlesex County, and I would use that responsibility to examine the evidence, test proposals against planning policy and ensure residents’ concerns receive a fair hearing.

I will not promise a particular vote before reviewing a file. I will promise preparation, independence and the courage to ask difficult questions—especially when an ordinary resident has no practical appeal route.

Question 3 of 5

What do you believe Strathroy-Caradoc Council is doing well, and what would you like to see done differently?

Council does important work supporting community organizations and advancing infrastructure. I would work with Council to improve how we use our decision-making time.

The Legion’s proposed downtown memorial crosswalk illustrates the issue. A relatively straightforward request with a clear cost estimate went to Council, then to staff, back to Council and then to an advisory committee before returning again.

I would have supported its approval at a safe location within the quoted budget.

Council should make straightforward decisions once, provide clear direction to staff and reserve repeated scrutiny for matters involving greater cost, risk or long-term consequences.

I would also ask staff to work with local Legion representatives and other communities to identify appropriate memorial opportunities within municipal jurisdiction.

We need a Council that works together, is deliberate and rigorous when the stakes are high, but decisive and efficient when the answer is straightforward.

Question 4 of 5

What are the most important issues facing Strathroy-Caradoc, and what would your priorities be if elected?

The most important challenge is managing growth without allowing infrastructure needs and long-term costs to outpace the municipality’s capacity.

My priorities would be reliable water, wastewater, roads and drainage; responsible land-use planning; protection of agricultural land and rural interests; and clear financial oversight of major projects.

Strathroy-Caradoc needs a current, publicly available asset-management plan that identifies the condition of municipal assets, service levels, lifecycle costs, funding gaps and risks.

Growth should proceed where servicing capacity is demonstrated and where development pays a fair share of the infrastructure it requires.

Council must also publish understandable information about project costs, financing, debt, reserves and future operating impacts before making major commitments.

I would bring an engineer’s discipline to these decisions: identify the facts, test assumptions, compare practical options and consider the long-term consequences for residents and taxpayers before voting.

Question 5 of 5

How would you ensure that the municipality’s communities are fairly represented at Council?

I took some time to look more closely at the different needs across our municipality.

Melbourne has municipal water but no municipal sewage system, which limits its growth. Rural residents face different issues, including gravel roads, drainage and access to services. Mount Brydges is growing quickly and faces significant water, wastewater and recreation decisions. Strathroy must manage continued growth, downtown renewal and major water, wastewater and transportation needs.

Fair representation cannot mean responding only to the voices heard most often or promising every community the same thing. It means understanding the evidence and setting priorities using clear, consistent criteria.

As Deputy Mayor, I would meet regularly with residents throughout the four-year term and remain close to local needs.

Different communities have different needs, but every resident should expect the same level of attention, transparency and accountability from Council.
